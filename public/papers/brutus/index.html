Brutus

Brutus was the pseudonym used by an anonymous Anti-Federalist writer during the debates over ratification of the U.S. Constitution. The authorship is often attributed to Robert Yates, a New York judge and delegate to the Constitutional Convention.

Brutus’s essays warned that the proposed Constitution would create a strong central government that could overpower state authority. He expressed concern about the lack of a Bill of Rights and feared the judiciary would become too powerful, undermining individual freedoms. Brutus believed that a large republic would lead to the erosion of liberty and democratic principles.

Brutus I

Argues that the proposed Constitution would create a consolidated national government, threatening individual liberties and state sovereignty.

Brutus II

Emphasizes the necessity of a Bill of Rights to safeguard individual freedoms against potential federal overreach.

Brutus III

Criticizes the apportionment of representation in Congress, asserting it disadvantages smaller states and lacks true proportionality.

Brutus IV

Expresses concern that the small size of the House of Representatives would lead to an elite ruling class detached from the populace.

Brutus IX

Advocates for clear limitations on governmental powers to prevent the erosion of individual rights and freedoms.

Brutus V

Warns that the Necessary and Proper Clause grants Congress expansive powers that could undermine state authority.

Brutus VI

Highlights the dangers of federal taxation powers potentially rendering state governments financially dependent and powerless.

Brutus VII

Continues the discussion on taxation, arguing that concurrent taxation powers would lead to conflicts detrimental to state functions.

Brutus VIII

Opposes the maintenance of a standing army in peacetime, fearing it could be used to suppress civil liberties.

Brutus X

Draws from historical examples to illustrate how standing armies have historically led to the downfall of free governments.

Brutus XI

Raises concerns about the federal judiciary’s potential to expand its power beyond constitutional limits.

Brutus XII

Argues that the federal courts would gradually encroach upon and diminish state judicial authority.

Brutus XIII

Questions the propriety of allowing individuals to sue states in federal courts, fearing it undermines state sovereignty.

Brutus XIV

Critiques the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court, suggesting it could overwhelm state courts and laws.

Brutus XV

Warns that the Supreme Court’s interpretative powers could effectively allow it to shape the Constitution to its will.

Brutus XV

Examines the Senate’s structure and powers, expressing concern over its potential to dominate the legislative process.
