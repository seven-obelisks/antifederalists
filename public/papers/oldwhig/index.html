An Old Whig

An Old Whig was the pseudonym used by an anonymous Anti-Federalist writer during the U.S. Constitution ratification debates. The authorship is often attributed to John Dickinson, a Pennsylvania statesman.

An Old Whig’s essays criticized the Constitution for centralizing power and diminishing state authority. He argued that the lack of a Bill of Rights left citizens vulnerable to government overreach. An Old Whig called for amendments to ensure the protection of individual liberties and a fair distribution of power between the states and the federal government.

An Old Whig I

Critiques the Constitution for centralizing too much power in the national government, which would undermine state sovereignty and jeopardize individual liberties.

An Old Whig II

Warns that the new government, being distant and removed from the people, would weaken representation and diminish individual influence in the political process.

An Old Whig III

Expresses concern that the creation of a standing army under federal control could be used to oppress states and the people, threatening the foundation of republican government.

An Old Whig IV

Argues that the executive branch under the proposed Constitution will become far too powerful, overshadowing the legislative branch and disrupting the intended balance of power.

An Old Whig V

Criticizes the absence of a Bill of Rights in the Constitution, emphasizing the need for explicit protections to safeguard individual freedoms from potential government overreach.

An Old Whig VI

Warns that the broad powers granted to the federal government, particularly in taxation and military control, could lead to unchecked federal power and undermine state authority.

An Old Whig VII

Warns that the Senate would become an aristocratic body, disconnected from the people and concentrated with too much power, undermining democratic principles.

An Old Whig VIII

Raises doubts about the effectiveness of the proposed checks and balances, arguing that they would be insufficient to prevent the concentration of power in the federal government.
